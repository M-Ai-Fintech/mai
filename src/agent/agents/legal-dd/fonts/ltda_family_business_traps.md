# Due Diligence Traps in Family-Owned Sociedades Limitadas (LTDA) in Brazil

Sociedades Limitadas (LTDA) represent the vast majority of middle-market target
companies in Brazil. Often operated as family businesses, they present unique
legal risks that require rigorous scrutiny during due diligence.

---

## 1. Mixing of Personal and Corporate Assets (_Confusão Patrimonial_)

In family-owned LTDAs, the boundary between the family’s personal finances and
the company’s treasury is frequently blurred.

- **Legal Risk (Art. 50, Civil Code):** _Confusão Patrimonial_ is the primary
  ground for the **Disregard of Corporate Entity** (_Desconsideração da
  Personalidade Jurídica_). If proven, the company's creditors can seize the
  personal assets of the partners, and vice versa (_desconsideração inversa_).
- **Red Flags to Audit:**
  1. **Personal Expenses Paid by the Company:** Checking accounts showing
     company funds used to pay for family members' private school tuition,
     luxury vehicles, personal credit cards, or home maintenance.
  2. **Informal Intercompany Loans (_Mútuos_):** Loans made from the company to
     family members (or sibling family companies) without written contracts,
     fixed repayment schedules, or interest at market rates.
  3. **Real Estate Overlap:** The company operating out of real estate owned by
     a partner without a formal lease agreement, or the family living in real
     estate owned by the company without paying rent (_comodato_).
- _DD Recommendation:_ Review the last 3 years of ledger accounts (specifically
  "Contas de Sócios" or "Mútuos Passivos/Ativos") and verify that all
  transactions are at arm's length.

---

## 2. Succession Conflicts and Heirs Entrance (_Apuração de Haveres_)

The death, divorce, or withdrawal of a patriarch/managing partner can severely
disrupt a family-owned LTDA if the Articles of Association (_Contrato Social_)
are poorly drafted.

- **The Default Rule (Art. 1,028, Civil Code):** If the _Contrato Social_ is
  silent, the death of a partner does **not** make their heirs partners.
  Instead, the deceased partner’s share must be liquidated (_apuração de
  haveres_), and the company must pay the value of the quotas to the heirs in
  cash within **90 days** (Art. 1,031, § 1º, Civil Code).
- **Liquidation Method (Art. 606, Code of Civil Procedure - CPC):** The quotas
  must be valued using a special balance sheet (_balanço de determinação_) based
  on the fair market value of assets and liabilities at the date of death or
  withdrawal.
- **The Trap:** A sudden obligation to pay out a deceased or divorced partner’s
  heirs at fair market value in 90 days can trigger a **fatal liquidity crisis**
  for a middle-market business.
- _DD Checklist:_
  - Does the _Contrato Social_ allow heirs to enter the company only upon
    consent of the surviving partners?
  - Does it establish a long-term installment plan (e.g., 60 monthly
    installments) for paying out _haveres_?
  - Are the valuation metrics for _haveres_ clearly defined (e.g., book value or
    historical EBITDA multiple) to avoid judicial disputes?

---

## 3. Quote Pledging (_Penhora de Quotas_)

- **Mechanism (Art. 861, CPC):** Creditors of a partner’s individual debts can
  request the judicial pledge (_penhora_) of that partner’s quotas in the LTDA,
  even if the _Contrato Social_ prohibits the transfer of quotas to third
  parties.
- **The Process:** The judge gives the company and the other partners the right
  to buy the quotas at their book value. If they cannot or do not buy them, the
  quotas are sold at a public auction, introducing an unwanted third-party
  competitor or hostile creditor into the family business.
- _DD Action:_ Run comprehensive litigation searches (_certidões de distribuição
  cível, execução fiscal e trabalhista_) not only against the target company but
  also against all **individual partners** holding more than 10% of the capital.

---

## 4. Exclusion of Partners for Just Cause (_Exclusão por Justa Causa_)

- **Legal Framework (Art. 1,085, Civil Code):** A partner who puts the company’s
  continuity at risk by committing acts of undeniable gravity can be excluded by
  a majority vote of the other partners (representing more than 50% of the
  capital), provided that:
  1. The exclusion is explicitly permitted in the _Contrato Social_.
  2. A general meeting is called, giving the accused partner a right of defense.
- **The 50/50 Deadlock Trap:** In family businesses where ownership is split
  50/50 between two siblings or branches, exclusion under Art. 1,085 is legally
  impossible without a long and costly judicial lawsuit. This leads to complete
  management paralysis.
- _DD Action:_ Analyze the equity distribution and check for deadlock-resolution
  mechanisms (such as shot-gun clauses or mandatory mediation/arbitration).

---

## 5. Lack of Professionalized Corporate Governance

Family LTDAs frequently ignore standard corporate formalities:

- **Large-Scale Limitadas (_Sociedades de Grande Porte_):** Under Law
  11.638/2007, an LTDA (or group under common control) with assets exceeding BRL
  240M or gross annual revenue exceeding BRL 300M in the previous fiscal year is
  classified as a "Grande Porte". They are legally required to have their annual
  financial statements audited by an independent auditor registered with the
  CVM. Many family-owned LTDAs meet these criteria but fail to perform audits.
- **Lack of Formal Minutes:** Family decisions are often made over informal
  discussions. The lack of formal partner resolutions (_Atas de
  Reunião/Assembleia de Sócios_) approving annual accounts can expose past
  transactions to challenges by dissenting family members (statute of
  limitations of 2 years under Art. 1,078 of Civil Code).
