# Due Diligence Traps in Family-Owned Sociedades Limitadas (LTDA) in Brazil

Sociedades Limitadas (LTDA) represent the vast majority of middle-market target companies in Brazil. Often operated as family businesses, they present unique legal risks that require rigorous scrutiny during due diligence.

---

## 1. Mixing of Personal and Corporate Assets (*Confusão Patrimonial*)
In family-owned LTDAs, the boundary between the family’s personal finances and the company’s treasury is frequently blurred.

*   **Legal Risk (Art. 50, Civil Code):** *Confusão Patrimonial* is the primary ground for the **Disregard of Corporate Entity** (*Desconsideração da Personalidade Jurídica*). If proven, the company's creditors can seize the personal assets of the partners, and vice versa (*desconsideração inversa*).
*   **Red Flags to Audit:**
    1.  **Personal Expenses Paid by the Company:** Checking accounts showing company funds used to pay for family members' private school tuition, luxury vehicles, personal credit cards, or home maintenance.
    2.  **Informal Intercompany Loans (*Mútuos*):** Loans made from the company to family members (or sibling family companies) without written contracts, fixed repayment schedules, or interest at market rates.
    3.  **Real Estate Overlap:** The company operating out of real estate owned by a partner without a formal lease agreement, or the family living in real estate owned by the company without paying rent (*comodato*).
*   *DD Recommendation:* Review the last 3 years of ledger accounts (specifically "Contas de Sócios" or "Mútuos Passivos/Ativos") and verify that all transactions are at arm's length.

---

## 2. Succession Conflicts and Heirs Entrance (*Apuração de Haveres*)
The death, divorce, or withdrawal of a patriarch/managing partner can severely disrupt a family-owned LTDA if the Articles of Association (*Contrato Social*) are poorly drafted.

*   **The Default Rule (Art. 1,028, Civil Code):** If the *Contrato Social* is silent, the death of a partner does **not** make their heirs partners. Instead, the deceased partner’s share must be liquidated (*apuração de haveres*), and the company must pay the value of the quotas to the heirs in cash within **90 days** (Art. 1,031, § 1º, Civil Code).
*   **Liquidation Method (Art. 606, Code of Civil Procedure - CPC):** The quotas must be valued using a special balance sheet (*balanço de determinação*) based on the fair market value of assets and liabilities at the date of death or withdrawal.
*   **The Trap:** A sudden obligation to pay out a deceased or divorced partner’s heirs at fair market value in 90 days can trigger a **fatal liquidity crisis** for a middle-market business.
*   *DD Checklist:*
    *   Does the *Contrato Social* allow heirs to enter the company only upon consent of the surviving partners?
    *   Does it establish a long-term installment plan (e.g., 60 monthly installments) for paying out *haveres*?
    *   Are the valuation metrics for *haveres* clearly defined (e.g., book value or historical EBITDA multiple) to avoid judicial disputes?

---

## 3. Quote Pledging (*Penhora de Quotas*)
*   **Mechanism (Art. 861, CPC):** Creditors of a partner’s individual debts can request the judicial pledge (*penhora*) of that partner’s quotas in the LTDA, even if the *Contrato Social* prohibits the transfer of quotas to third parties.
*   **The Process:** The judge gives the company and the other partners the right to buy the quotas at their book value. If they cannot or do not buy them, the quotas are sold at a public auction, introducing an unwanted third-party competitor or hostile creditor into the family business.
*   *DD Action:* Run comprehensive litigation searches (*certidões de distribuição cível, execução fiscal e trabalhista*) not only against the target company but also against all **individual partners** holding more than 10% of the capital.

---

## 4. Exclusion of Partners for Just Cause (*Exclusão por Justa Causa*)
*   **Legal Framework (Art. 1,085, Civil Code):** A partner who puts the company’s continuity at risk by committing acts of undeniable gravity can be excluded by a majority vote of the other partners (representing more than 50% of the capital), provided that:
    1.  The exclusion is explicitly permitted in the *Contrato Social*.
    2.  A general meeting is called, giving the accused partner a right of defense.
*   **The 50/50 Deadlock Trap:** In family businesses where ownership is split 50/50 between two siblings or branches, exclusion under Art. 1,085 is legally impossible without a long and costly judicial lawsuit. This leads to complete management paralysis.
*   *DD Action:* Analyze the equity distribution and check for deadlock-resolution mechanisms (such as shot-gun clauses or mandatory mediation/arbitration).

---

## 5. Lack of Professionalized Corporate Governance
Family LTDAs frequently ignore standard corporate formalities:
*   **Large-Scale Limitadas (*Sociedades de Grande Porte*):** Under Law 11.638/2007, an LTDA (or group under common control) with assets exceeding BRL 240M or gross annual revenue exceeding BRL 300M in the previous fiscal year is classified as a "Grande Porte". They are legally required to have their annual financial statements audited by an independent auditor registered with the CVM. Many family-owned LTDAs meet these criteria but fail to perform audits.
*   **Lack of Formal Minutes:** Family decisions are often made over informal discussions. The lack of formal partner resolutions (*Atas de Reunião/Assembleia de Sócios*) approving annual accounts can expose past transactions to challenges by dissenting family members (statute of limitations of 2 years under Art. 1,078 of Civil Code).
