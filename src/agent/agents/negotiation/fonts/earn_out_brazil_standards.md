# Earn-Out Clauses: Legal and Financial Standards in Brazil

This document outlines the best practices for structuring, calculating, and
protecting earn-out mechanisms in Brazilian M&A transactions, complying with
local accounting standards (CPC) and civil law principles.

---

## 1. Defining EBITDA Targets

The use of Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization
(EBITDA) is the most common metric for earn-out structures. However, using
unadjusted EBITDA leads to accounting disputes.

### A. The Principle of Consistent Application (_Consistência de Práticas_)

The SPA must mandate that post-closing financial performance is measured using
the exact same accounting policies, estimates, and methods used by the target
company in its historical, audited pre-closing financial statements.

- _Reference:_ Align with **CPC 23 (Políticas Contábeis, Mudança de Estimativa e
  Retificação de Erro)**.

### B. Standard EBITDA Adjustments (Adjusted EBITDA)

The contract must define the calculation of "Earn-Out EBITDA" by excluding:

1. **Corporate Overhead Allocations:** Fees, shared service center (CSC) costs,
   or management fees charged by the Buyer’s parent group.
2. **M&A Transaction Costs:** Legal, financial advisory, and audit fees incurred
   in connection with the acquisition.
3. **Intercompany Transactions:** Revenues or expenses generated with the
   Buyer’s other subsidiaries that are not on strictly arm’s length terms
   (_preços de transferência_).
4. **Restructuring and Integration Costs:** Expenses related to employee layoffs
   or IT system migrations initiated by the Buyer post-closing.
5. **Changes in Tax Regimes:** Adjustments for changes in tax planning (e.g.,
   transitioning the target from _Lucro Presumido_ to _Lucro Real_).

## 2. Preventing Post-Closing Accounting Manipulation (_Gerenciamento de Resultados_)

Buyers may seek to artificially depress EBITDA during the earn-out period to
minimize payouts, while Sellers (if they remain in management) may seek to
inflate it.

### A. Common Manipulation Tactics

- _Buyer Tactics:_ Shifting target customer contracts to other group entities,
  delaying the billing of sales, accelerating the booking of vendor invoices,
  capitalizing operational expenses as CapEx, or increasing bad debt provisions
  (_PECLD - Provisão para Perdas em Créditos de Liquidação Duvidosa_).
- _Seller Tactics:_ Delaying maintenance CapEx, offering aggressive customer
  discounts to pull forward future revenues, or underinvesting in marketing.

### B. Contractual Safeguards

- **Information Rights:** The Seller must have real-time read access to the
  target's ERP system (e.g., SAP, Totvs), monthly management accounts, trial
  balances (_balancetes_), and tax filings.
- **Audit Rights:** The Seller has the right to appoint an independent audit
  firm to review the Buyer's earn-out calculation statement within a set
  timeframe (typically 30 to 45 business days).

### C. Dispute Resolution via Perícia Contábil

If the parties disagree on the earn-out calculation, the dispute should bypass
courts and be referred to a pre-selected **Independent Accountant** (_Perito
Contador_ or a "Big Four" firm not working for either party). The expert’s
determination is final, binding, and serves as an extrajudicial execution title
(_título executivo extrajudicial_).

## 3. Resolving Buyer-Side Operational Interference

A critical point of litigation in Brazil is the Buyer's post-closing operational
interference which blocks the Target's ability to hit its financial targets.

### A. Legal Foundation: Objective Good Faith & Imputed Fulfillment

- **Art. 113 & 422 of the Brazilian Civil Code:** Mandate that parties act in
  accordance with objective good faith (_boa-fé objetiva_) during the execution
  and performance of contracts.
- **Art. 129 of the Brazilian Civil Code:**
  > _"Reputa-se verificada, quanto aos efeitos jurídicos, a condição cujo
  > implemento for maliciosamente obstado pela parte a quem desaproveitaria..."_
  > If the Buyer acts in bad faith or takes actions that intentionally prevent
  > the target from hitting its earn-out metrics, the condition is legally
  > deemed as **fulfilled**, and the Buyer must pay the maximum earn-out value.

### B. Operational Covenants (_Obrigações de Fazer e Não Fazer_)

The SPA should include covenants governing the operation of the target during
the earn-out period:

1. **Ordinary Course of Business:** The Buyer must operate the target in the
   ordinary course of business (_curso ordinário dos negócios_), maintaining
   historical operational standards.
2. **Funding Obligation:** The Buyer must provide a pre-agreed minimum level of
   working capital and CapEx funding to support the target’s approved business
   plan.
3. **Non-Diversion of Resources:** Prohibition against transferring key
   executive talent, technical staff, or intellectual property away from the
   target to other Buyer entities.

### C. Accelerator Clauses (_Cláusulas de Aceleração_)

The earn-out is accelerated and becomes immediately payable at the maximum
payout level if:

- The Buyer terminates the Seller/Founder without Just Cause (_Justa Causa_
  under Art. 482 of the CLT) during the earn-out period.
- The Buyer sells or transfers a controlling stake in the target company or its
  material assets to a third party.
- The Buyer breaches any of the operational covenants and fails to cure the
  breach within 30 days.
