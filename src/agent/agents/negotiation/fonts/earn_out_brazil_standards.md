# Earn-Out Clauses: Legal and Financial Standards in Brazil

This document outlines the best practices for structuring, calculating, and protecting earn-out mechanisms in Brazilian M&A transactions, complying with local accounting standards (CPC) and civil law principles.

---

## 1. Defining EBITDA Targets

The use of Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization (EBITDA) is the most common metric for earn-out structures. However, using unadjusted EBITDA leads to accounting disputes.

### A. The Principle of Consistent Application (*Consistência de Práticas*)
The SPA must mandate that post-closing financial performance is measured using the exact same accounting policies, estimates, and methods used by the target company in its historical, audited pre-closing financial statements.
*   *Reference:* Align with **CPC 23 (Políticas Contábeis, Mudança de Estimativa e Retificação de Erro)**.

### B. Standard EBITDA Adjustments (Adjusted EBITDA)
The contract must define the calculation of "Earn-Out EBITDA" by excluding:
1.  **Corporate Overhead Allocations:** Fees, shared service center (CSC) costs, or management fees charged by the Buyer’s parent group.
2.  **M&A Transaction Costs:** Legal, financial advisory, and audit fees incurred in connection with the acquisition.
3.  **Intercompany Transactions:** Revenues or expenses generated with the Buyer’s other subsidiaries that are not on strictly arm’s length terms (*preços de transferência*).
4.  **Restructuring and Integration Costs:** Expenses related to employee layoffs or IT system migrations initiated by the Buyer post-closing.
5.  **Changes in Tax Regimes:** Adjustments for changes in tax planning (e.g., transitioning the target from *Lucro Presumido* to *Lucro Real*).

## 2. Preventing Post-Closing Accounting Manipulation (*Gerenciamento de Resultados*)

Buyers may seek to artificially depress EBITDA during the earn-out period to minimize payouts, while Sellers (if they remain in management) may seek to inflate it.

### A. Common Manipulation Tactics
*   *Buyer Tactics:* Shifting target customer contracts to other group entities, delaying the billing of sales, accelerating the booking of vendor invoices, capitalizing operational expenses as CapEx, or increasing bad debt provisions (*PECLD - Provisão para Perdas em Créditos de Liquidação Duvidosa*).
*   *Seller Tactics:* Delaying maintenance CapEx, offering aggressive customer discounts to pull forward future revenues, or underinvesting in marketing.

### B. Contractual Safeguards
*   **Information Rights:** The Seller must have real-time read access to the target's ERP system (e.g., SAP, Totvs), monthly management accounts, trial balances (*balancetes*), and tax filings.
*   **Audit Rights:** The Seller has the right to appoint an independent audit firm to review the Buyer's earn-out calculation statement within a set timeframe (typically 30 to 45 business days).

### C. Dispute Resolution via Perícia Contábil
If the parties disagree on the earn-out calculation, the dispute should bypass courts and be referred to a pre-selected **Independent Accountant** (*Perito Contador* or a "Big Four" firm not working for either party). The expert’s determination is final, binding, and serves as an extrajudicial execution title (*título executivo extrajudicial*).

## 3. Resolving Buyer-Side Operational Interference

A critical point of litigation in Brazil is the Buyer's post-closing operational interference which blocks the Target's ability to hit its financial targets.

### A. Legal Foundation: Objective Good Faith & Imputed Fulfillment
*   **Art. 113 & 422 of the Brazilian Civil Code:** Mandate that parties act in accordance with objective good faith (*boa-fé objetiva*) during the execution and performance of contracts.
*   **Art. 129 of the Brazilian Civil Code:**
    > *"Reputa-se verificada, quanto aos efeitos jurídicos, a condição cujo implemento for maliciosamente obstado pela parte a quem desaproveitaria..."*
    If the Buyer acts in bad faith or takes actions that intentionally prevent the target from hitting its earn-out metrics, the condition is legally deemed as **fulfilled**, and the Buyer must pay the maximum earn-out value.

### B. Operational Covenants (*Obrigações de Fazer e Não Fazer*)
The SPA should include covenants governing the operation of the target during the earn-out period:
1.  **Ordinary Course of Business:** The Buyer must operate the target in the ordinary course of business (*curso ordinário dos negócios*), maintaining historical operational standards.
2.  **Funding Obligation:** The Buyer must provide a pre-agreed minimum level of working capital and CapEx funding to support the target’s approved business plan.
3.  **Non-Diversion of Resources:** Prohibition against transferring key executive talent, technical staff, or intellectual property away from the target to other Buyer entities.

### C. Accelerator Clauses (*Cláusulas de Aceleração*)
The earn-out is accelerated and becomes immediately payable at the maximum payout level if:
*   The Buyer terminates the Seller/Founder without Just Cause (*Justa Causa* under Art. 482 of the CLT) during the earn-out period.
*   The Buyer sells or transfers a controlling stake in the target company or its material assets to a third party.
*   The Buyer breaches any of the operational covenants and fails to cure the breach within 30 days.
