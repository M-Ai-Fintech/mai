# Key Person Risk Matrix: Brazilian Middle-Market Firms

## 1. Executive Summary

In Brazilian middle-market companies (revenues between BRL 30M and BRL 300M),
business operations, credit access, client relationships, and regulatory permits
are heavily concentrated in the hands of the original founders or key
executives. This centralization creates significant operational and structural
risks for incoming financial sponsors or strategic buyers.

This document establishes a formalized Key Person Risk Matrix, evaluating
critical dependency vectors, behavioral red flags, and legal mitigation
mechanisms under Brazilian jurisprudence.

## 3. Structural and Legal Mitigation Mechanisms under Brazilian Law

### 3.1 Non-Compete Covenants (_Cláusula de Não-Concorrência_)

To protect the investment, buyers must restrict the departing founder from
launching a competing business. Under the jurisprudence of the Superior Court of
Justice (STJ), a non-compete covenant is only enforceable if it meets four
cumulative requirements:

1. **Temporal Limitation:** The restriction must have a defined duration
   (standard market practice in Brazil is 3 to 5 years).
2. **Geographical Limitation:** The territorial scope must be explicitly defined
   (e.g., "within the national territory" or "within the states where the target
   operates").
3. **Scope of Activity:** The restricted segment must match the exact business
   of the target.
4. **Mandatory Financial Compensation:** Under STJ rulings, the non-compete must
   be compensated. If the contract does not stipulate a specific payout (either
   integrated into the share price or paid in monthly installments during the
   restriction period), the clause is void, allowing the founder to compete
   immediately.

### 3.2 Key Person Insurance (_Seguro de Vida Homem-Chave_)

- **Mechanism:** A corporate-owned life and disability insurance policy where
  the company is the sole beneficiary.
- **Tax Treatment:** Under Brazilian tax law (_Lucro Real_ regime), premiums
  paid by the company for key person insurance can be deducted as administrative
  expenses (Article 311 of RIR/2018), reducing corporate income tax (IRPJ/CSLL),
  provided the policy has no redemption features during the employment term.

### 3.3 Earn-Out and Escrow Structuring

- **Earn-Outs:** Structure 20% to 50% of the Enterprise Value (EV) as a deferred
  payment contingent on two criteria:
  1. Financial Performance: Meeting target EBITDA thresholds.
  2. Operational Retention: The founder remaining active during a transition
     period (typically 12 to 24 months) as an executive director or senior
     advisor.
- **Escrow Accounts:** Set aside 10% to 20% of the purchase price in a blocked
  bank account (_conta escrow_) for 3 to 5 years to cover indemnification claims
  (tax, labor, and civil contingencies) discovered post-closing. Release of
  funds is scheduled periodically.

### 3.4 Professionalization and Transition of Control

- **Advisory Boards (_Conselho Consultivo_):** Transition the founder from a CEO
  role to an advisory board seat. This honors their legacy, keeps their network
  accessible, and strips them of daily veto powers.
- **Second-Tier Retention:** Identify key middle-managers (e.g., CFO, CTO, Head
  of Sales) and bind them to the target post-closing using structured retention
  bonuses or Phantom Stock plans designed to vest over 36 months.
