# Tasks & Approvals

## 1. Recommended product naming

Use:

* **Global navigation:** `Tasks & Approvals`
* **Page title:** `Tasks & Approvals`
* **Default view:** `My Work`
* **Actionable unit:** `Work Item`
* **Execution unit:** `Task`
* **Decision unit:** `Approval`
* **Assessment unit:** `Review`
* **Team-level view:** `Team Queue`
* **Historical record:** `Decision History`

The global navigation should prioritize **Tasks & Approvals** over Notifications because tasks and approvals represent required work, while notifications merely report events. 

The screen is the principal banker-facing surface of the **Deal Workflow Orchestrator**, which assigns tasks, monitors exceptions and routes material outputs for human approval. 

---

# 2. Screen definition

**Tasks & Approvals** is the institution-level action workspace where users review, perform, delegate and decide transaction work across every deal they are authorized to access.

It consolidates work generated by:

* Deal participants;
* Dossier review;
* Document synchronization;
* Diligence findings;
* Valuation scenarios;
* Counterparty screening;
* Negotiation rounds;
* Closing requirements;
* Agentic missions;
* Institution policies;
* External information requests;
* Approval rules.

It should answer:

1. **What requires my action now?**
2. **Which items are most consequential?**
3. **What is overdue or blocking a transaction?**
4. **Why was the item assigned to me?**
5. **What evidence and context should I review?**
6. **What authority is required?**
7. **What changes if I approve, reject or complete it?**
8. **Which work is waiting on someone else?**
9. **Which decisions have been invalidated by new information?**
10. **What should I open next?**

Its central operating sequence is:

> **Exception or requirement → Work item → Exact transaction context → Human action → State change → Audit record**

This is the canonical product implementation of the exception-to-decision flow already defined for M&Ai. 

---

# 3. Primary intention

## Operational intention

Create one reliable action queue across the user’s deals without forcing the banker to inspect every transaction screen manually.

## User intention

A banker should be able to say:

> “I know what I must do, why it matters, what is due, what evidence supports it and what consequence my action will have.”

## Product intention

The screen should prove that M&Ai does not merely generate analysis.

It routes transaction outputs into controlled human workflows.

This is essential to the product’s **decision-grade layer**, which connects evidence, deterministic engines and human approvals. 

---

# 4. Strategic product role

Tasks & Approvals is the connective tissue between every product module.

```text
Documents
  ↓
Evidence change
  ↓
Dossier review
  ↓
Diligence finding
  ↓
Valuation treatment
  ↓
Human approval
  ↓
Negotiation or Closing consequence
```

Without this screen, users must discover work by:

* Opening individual deals;
* reading notifications;
* checking email;
* monitoring chat;
* inspecting agent activity;
* asking colleagues;
* maintaining an external task list.

Tasks & Approvals should become the canonical answer to:

> **“What requires human judgment or intervention?”**

---

# 5. What the screen is not

The screen should not become:

* A generic to-do list;
* A notification inbox;
* A project-management board;
* A team productivity scoreboard;
* A substitute for the authoritative deal screen;
* A generic activity feed;
* An email inbox;
* A workflow-administration console;
* A list of every system event;
* A mass-approval surface;
* A blind “approve all” queue;
* A calendar application;
* An autonomous prioritization engine;
* A permanent chat interface;
* A place where material decisions occur without evidence.

The central question is not:

> “How many tasks are open?”

It is:

> **“Which human action is required, with what authority and transaction consequence?”**

## Command Center versus Tasks & Approvals

The Command Center answers:

> What deserves attention across my deals?

Tasks & Approvals answers:

> What exact action must I take?

## Notifications versus Tasks

A notification may state:

> A new document version was synchronized.

A task may state:

> Review the four approved facts affected by the new document version.

## Tasks versus authoritative screens

Tasks & Approvals should provide enough context to triage and understand the action.

The complete decision should normally occur in:

* Target Dossier;
* Documents;
* Diligence;
* Valuation;
* Buyer Universe;
* Negotiation;
* Closing.

# 7. Fundamental distinction: tasks, reviews and approvals

## 7.1 Task

A unit of work that must be completed.

Examples:

* Classify an unrecognized document;
* obtain a missing tax certificate;
* reconcile a debt schedule;
* prepare a client brief;
* update a buyer profile.

A task typically ends with:

> **Complete**

## 7.2 Review

An assessment that must be performed before work may progress.

Examples:

* Review an extracted Dossier fact;
* review a new document version;
* review EBITDA normalization;
* review a proposal extraction;
* review closing evidence.

A review typically ends with:

* Accept;
* request changes;
* mark insufficient;
* escalate.

## 7.3 Approval

A decision that authorizes or rejects a material state change.

Examples:

* Approve a valuation treatment;
* approve a shortlist;
* authorize outreach;
* approve a counterproposal;
* waive a closing condition;
* authorize closing.

An approval typically ends with:

* Approve;
* approve with conditions;
* reject;
* request changes;
* abstain or recuse.

## UX rule

Do not use the word **Approve** for ordinary task completion.

# 8. Notifications, alerts and work items

The product should maintain four distinct concepts.

| Concept                | Meaning                                                 | Typical destination           |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Task**               | The user must perform work                              | Tasks & Approvals             |
| **Review or approval** | The user must assess or decide                          | Tasks & Approvals             |
| **Notification**       | Something happened                                      | Notification Center           |
| **System alert**       | Reliability, security or integration requires attention | Shell alert or administration |

Example:

### Notification

> Document synchronization completed.

### Task

> Review four affected dossier facts.

### Approval

> Approve the revised customer-concentration treatment.

### System alert

> Document-source authorization expired.

Dismissing a notification must never complete a task or approval.

# 9. Primary users

## 9.1 Analyst

Needs to:

* See assigned work;
* complete evidence collection;
* prepare reviews;
* monitor submitted work;
* respond to requested changes;
* understand blockers.

## 9.2 Associate

Needs to:

* Review analyst outputs;
* reassign tasks;
* resolve exceptions;
* submit findings and scenarios;
* manage workstream queues;
* coordinate deadlines.

## 9.3 Vice President

Needs to:

* Review material findings;
* approve within delegated authority;
* monitor overdue work;
* identify transaction blockers;
* escalate unresolved issues;
* coordinate senior decisions.

## 9.4 Managing Director or Partner

Needs:

* High-materiality decisions;
* major deadline risks;
* cross-deal approvals;
* material commercial deviations;
* closing blockers;
* concise evidence and alternatives.

## 9.5 Specialist reviewer

A legal, tax, accounting or operational specialist needs:

* Workstream-specific reviews;
* source evidence;
* explicit questions;
* due dates;
* clear decision authority.

## 9.6 Deal administrator

Needs to:

* Correct assignment;
* monitor workflow health;
* identify orphaned work;
* manage escalation;
* resolve invalid routing.

They should not gain transaction-decision authority merely because they administer workflows.

## 9.7 Client or external approver

May receive:

* Restricted approval request;
* selected evidence;
* defined alternatives;
* deadline;
* explicit decision.

They should not enter the full internal Tasks & Approvals workspace by default.

# 11. Core conceptual model

The screen should be built around six layers.

## Layer 1 — Trigger

The event or requirement that created the work.

Example:

> Customer-revenue document updated.

## Layer 2 — Work item

The task, review or approval assigned to a person or group.

## Layer 3 — Context

The exact deal object, evidence and current state.

## Layer 4 — Authority

Who may perform or decide the action.

## Layer 5 — Consequence

What changes or becomes unblocked after action.

## Layer 6 — Audit

Who acted, when, with what rationale and on which version.

# 13. Work-item lifecycle

A common lifecycle should support:

* Draft;
* open;
* assigned;
* in progress;
* waiting on user;
* waiting on external party;
* blocked;
* ready for review;
* awaiting approval;
* completed;
* rejected;
* cancelled;
* withdrawn;
* invalidated;
* superseded;
* expired;
* archived.

Not every work-item type uses every state.

---

# 14. Task lifecycle

Recommended:

```text
Open
  ↓
Assigned
  ↓
In progress
  ↓
Waiting, when applicable
  ↓
Ready for review
  ↓
Completed
```

Alternative outcomes:

* Blocked;
* cancelled;
* superseded;
* expired.

## Completion rule

A task should complete only when its configured completion requirement is satisfied.

Examples:

* Evidence attached;
* reviewer accepted;
* linked source available;
* required fields complete;
* external response reconciled.

---

# 15. Approval lifecycle

Recommended:

```text
Draft
  ↓
Submitted
  ↓
Assigned
  ↓
Under review
  ↓
Approved / Approved with conditions / Rejected
```

Alternative outcomes:

* Changes requested;
* withdrawn;
* expired;
* invalidated;
* superseded;
* recused.

## Approval immutability

An approval decision should not be edited.

A correction creates:

* A new approval request;
* an invalidation record;
* or a superseding decision.

---

# 16. Review lifecycle

Recommended:

* Assigned;
* not started;
* in review;
* clarification requested;
* accepted;
* changes requested;
* insufficient evidence;
* escalated;
* superseded.

A review may lead to an approval request, but it is not automatically an approval.

3. **Tasks**

Items across an authorized team or workstream.

# 20. Operational summary

Use no more than five summary controls.

| Summary        | Example | Meaning                            |
| -------------- | ------: | ---------------------------------- |
| Assigned to me |   **9** | All unresolved assigned work       |
| Approvals      |   **3** | Decisions requiring authority      |
| Overdue        |   **2** | Past the current due date          |
| Blockers       |   **1** | Preventing a material workflow     |
| Waiting        |   **4** | Work paused on another participant |

Each summary should filter the queue.

## Avoid

Do not use:

* Personal productivity score;
* average approval speed as a hero;
* team ranking;
* total completed tasks;
* “AI efficiency” score;
* gamified streaks.

# 24. Materiality, severity, urgency and priority

The screen must distinguish four concepts.

## Materiality

Magnitude of transaction effect.

Example:

> Changes valuation midpoint by R$ 42 mi.

## Severity

Risk or issue seriousness.

Example:

> High-severity customer concentration finding.

## Urgency

Time available.

Example:

> Due in four hours.

## Priority

Explicit team ordering.

Example:

> Deal Lead marked as Priority 1.

A high-severity finding may not be immediately due.

A low-severity administrative task may become urgent before closing.

---

# 25. Explainable prioritization

The product may recommend an order, but the reason should be visible.

Example:

> **Recommended next**

Reasons:

* Blocks valuation approval;
* due today;
* affects the active negotiation range;
* assigned to you.

Do not use:

> AI priority score: 94

## Ordering policy

A deterministic default may prioritize:

1. Critical blockers;
2. material approvals due soon;
3. overdue required work;
4. items blocking others;
5. upcoming routine work.

Users may change the sort.

# 35. Decision actions

Recommended approval actions:

* Approve;
* approve with conditions;
* request changes;
* reject;
* escalate;
* abstain;
* recuse;
* delegate review, when permitted.

## UX rule

Do not show every action with equal visual emphasis.

Recommended hierarchy:

* Primary: Approve or complete;
* secondary: Request changes;
* tertiary: Reject, recuse, escalate.

Destructive or consequential actions require confirmation.

---

# 36. Approve with conditions

Conditional approval should require:

* Condition;
* owner;
* due date;
* whether it blocks activation;
* evidence requirement;
* authority.

Example:

> **Approved subject to Legal confirmation**

Condition:

> Verify that the earn-out audit-rights clause is included.

Consequence:

> Commercial response remains blocked until the condition is satisfied.

The condition should create a linked task automatically.

---

# 37. Request changes

A request for changes should capture:

* Required change;
* reason;
* assigned owner;
* due date;
* whether prior review remains valid;
* impacted object.

Example:

> Revise the selected multiple range to exclude two non-comparable companies and rerun scenario v4.

The work item returns to the preparer.

---

# 38. Rejection

A rejection should require:

* Reason;
* whether the object remains in draft;
* whether an alternative should be prepared;
* whether escalation is required.

Example:

> **Valuation treatment rejected**

Reason:

> Customer risk is already reflected in both revenue growth and the selected comparable multiple.

Consequence:

> Scenario v4 remains draft and requires recalculation.

---

# 39. Abstain and recuse

## Abstain

The user intentionally does not support or reject the decision.

Appropriate for:

* Committee vote;
* incomplete opinion;
* policy-defined quorum.

## Recuse

The user cannot participate due to:

* Conflict;
* independence requirement;
* relationship;
* lack of authority.

Recusal should trigger reassignment or escalation.

---

# 40. Decision rationale

Material approvals should require a concise rationale.

The system may suggest a summary based on the reviewed context, but the approver should confirm it.

Example:

> Approved because the updated customer schedule is current, the threshold breach is supported and the proposed treatment does not double count the identified risk.

Avoid mandatory long-form essays for routine approvals.

---

# 41. Evidence summary

The detail panel should show:

* Number of current sources;
* primary sources;
* supporting sources;
* contradictory sources;
* stale sources;
* missing evidence.

Example:

> **Evidence**

* 2 primary sources;
* 1 supporting source;
* no unresolved contradiction;
* current through 27 July 2026.

Primary action:

> Inspect evidence

---

# 43. Quick action boundary

Some items may be completed inside Tasks & Approvals.

Appropriate:

* Acknowledge assignment;
* change due date;
* delegate routine work;
* accept a low-risk classification;
* complete an administrative task;
* approve a fully contextual low-materiality item.

Inappropriate:

* Approve a material valuation scenario from a one-line preview;
* authorize outreach without viewing restrictions;
* waive a closing condition without evidence;
* authorize closing from the queue;
* release external communications blindly.

---

# 44. Focus Review mode

A focused sequential review mode can improve high-volume work.

## Recommended behavior

* One work item at a time;
* complete evidence and context;
* keyboard navigation;
* next-item preview;
* no skipped required warning;
* progress through selected queue.

Example:

> **Approval 2 of 5**

Controls:

* Open full context;
* request changes;
* approve;
* next item.

## UX rule

Focus Review must not become rapid-fire blind approval.

---

# 45. Work-item dependencies

An item may depend on:

* Another task;
* external evidence;
* source synchronization;
* specialist review;
* approval;
* date;
* permission;
* system integration.

## Example

> **Blocked**

> Valuation approval cannot proceed until EBITDA normalization review is complete.

The dependency should be clickable.

# 46. Downstream consequence

The screen should show what the item blocks or changes.

Example:

> **Completing this review will unblock**

* Risk-adjusted scenario v4;
* valuation report generation;
* negotiation reference range.

Example:

> **Approving this item will update**

* Deal Overview;
* current valuation treatment;
* audit record;
* assigned users’ notifications.

---

# 47. Waiting states

Recommended waiting reasons:

* Waiting on external party;
* waiting on evidence;
* waiting on reviewer;
* waiting on approval;
* waiting on system;
* waiting until date;
* waiting on dependency.

A waiting item should show:

* Who or what is being waited on;
* since when;
* expected date;
* escalation option;
* whether the user still has an action.

---

# 48. Overdue work

An item becomes overdue when its current due date passes without resolution.

The screen should show:

* Original due date;
* current due date;
* number of extensions;
* reason;
* consequence;
* escalation state.

## UX rule

Overdue does not automatically mean critical.

A low-impact administrative task may be overdue.

A not-yet-overdue closing blocker may be much more consequential.

---

# 49. Due-date changes

Changing a due date should require:

* New date and time;
* time zone;
* reason;
* authority, when material;
* affected milestone.

Example:

> Moving this review to 2 August places the client committee pack at risk.

Actions:

* Change deadline;
* retain deadline;
* escalate.

---

# 50. Time zones

Deadlines should be stored precisely and displayed with the relevant time zone.

Example:

> 31 jul. 2026 · 17:00 BRT

Where participants work across locations, show:

> 31 jul. · 17:00 BRT / 16:00 EDT

Avoid ambiguous:

> Tomorrow afternoon

for formal approval deadlines.

# 51. Delegation

Users may delegate tasks when policy permits.

Required fields:

* New assignee;
* reason;
* retained owner, if applicable;
* due date;
* handoff note;
* authority impact.

## Important distinction

### Task delegation

Transfers execution responsibility.

### Approval reassignment

Transfers decision responsibility only when policy allows.

A user must not delegate an approval to someone lacking authority.

The domain should distinguish:

* Assignee;
* accountable owner;
* reviewer;
* approver;
* watchers.

Example:

> Felipe Rocha performs the analysis.
> Ana Costa remains accountable for submitting it.
> Marina Alves approves it.

Delegating execution should not silently remove accountable ownership.

Assigned to:

# 58. Approval authority

Authority may depend on:

* Work-item type;
* deal role;
* monetary threshold;
* percentage movement;
* workstream;
* transaction phase;
* external commitment;
* jurisdiction;
* institution policy;
* client mandate.

## Example

> Valuation treatments with an impact above R$ 25 mi require VP approval.

> Complete valuation scenarios require Deal Lead approval.

> Closing waivers require Partner, client and counsel approval.

# 59. Separation of duties

The product should support policies such as:

* Preparer cannot be final approver;
* agent-generated output requires human reviewer;
* payment preparer cannot complete both verification steps;
* counsel review is required before term-sheet circulation;
* Deal Lead cannot approve a restricted conflict alone.

## State

> **Self-approval not permitted**

Action:

> Assign eligible approver

Example:

  ↓

  ↓
Deal Lead approval
  ↓
Client approval

Each stage should have:

* Assigned authority;
* state;
* decision;
* timestamp;
* conditions.

# 61. Parallel approvals

Some decisions require independent parallel approval.

Example:

> Closing readiness requires:

* Deal Lead;
* client principal;
* legal counsel;
* funds-flow reviewer.

The approval is complete only when the configured rule is satisfied.

## Possible rules

* All required;
* quorum;
* one of group;
* sequential;
* conditional.

The rule should be visible.

# 62. Approval quorum

For committee decisions, show:

* Eligible voters;
* votes recorded;
* required quorum;
* abstentions;
* conflicts;
* current outcome.

Example:

> **2 of 3 approvals recorded**

Required:

> 3 approvals, with at least one Partner.

Do not expose secret-ballot detail where policy prohibits it.

---

# 63. Conditional approvals

A conditional approval remains unresolved until its conditions are satisfied.

State example:

> **Approved with 2 open conditions**

Conditions:

* Legal clause verification;
* updated source attachment.

The parent approval should not be presented as fully effective until policy permits.

---

# 64. Decision invalidation

An approval should be invalidated when a material dependency changes.

Examples:

* Source document updated;
* Dossier fact superseded;
* valuation scenario recalculated;
* proposal terms changed;
* payment instructions changed;
* closing condition reopened;
* authority changed.

## Example

> **Approval invalidated**

Reason:

> The selected comparable-company set changed after approval.

Required:

> Review scenario v5.

The historical approval remains preserved for the prior version.

---

# 65. Stale work items

A work item may become stale when:

* Its source changed;
* its object was superseded;
* its required decision was completed elsewhere;
* its assignee lost access;
* its deadline became irrelevant;
* a newer request replaced it.

The system should automatically propose:

* Refresh;
* supersede;
* cancel;
* reopen;
* route to new context.

---

# 66. Duplicate work prevention

The system should avoid creating several tasks for the same required action.

Example:

* Agent creates a finding-review task;
* workflow template creates a review task;
* user manually assigns another review.

The product should detect:

> **Possible duplicate work item**

Actions:

* Merge;
* link;
* keep separate with rationale.

---

# 67. Comments and collaboration

Each item should support:

* Comment;
* mention;
* ask question;
* attach evidence;
* request specialist review;
* resolve discussion.

Comments should remain secondary to the structured task or decision.

Do not turn each work item into an uncontrolled chat channel.

Users may follow a work item without owning it.

* invalidation.

# 76. Agentic workflow integration

A human-in-the-loop agent workflow should create a structured approval request when it reaches a material boundary.

Example:

```text

  ↓

  ↓
Workflow suspended
  ↓
Approval appears in Tasks & Approvals
  ↓
Human reviews authoritative context
  ↓
Decision recorded
  ↓

```

This should replace vague:

# 77. Workflow resume behavior

After approval:

* The approval decision is persisted;
* exact object version is recorded;
* the suspended workflow resumes;
* only authorized next steps execute;
* downstream status updates;
* audit record is created.

After rejection:

* Workflow stops or returns to a revision step;
* no material state change occurs;
* a new task may be created.

---

# 78. No fabricated completion

An agent or engine run completing does not mean the associated work item is complete.

Example:

> Valuation calculation completed.

The approval remains:

> Awaiting human review.

Similarly:

> Document uploaded

does not mean:

> Missing-evidence task completed.

The task closes only after its completion policy is met.

* Assign owner;
* change due date;
* add watcher;
* send reminder;
* mark routine administrative tasks complete;
* export selected items;
* apply workstream.

* Accept non-material classifications;
* close duplicated routine tasks;
* move several items to waiting.

Inappropriate bulk actions:

* Approve material valuation changes;
* authorize several outreach actions;
* waive multiple conditions;
* approve closing;
* reject several unrelated decisions without individual rationale.

# 97. Concurrency and decision locking

Actions:

* Refresh review;
* compare changes;
* discard outdated decision draft.

Never accept a decision against an obsolete object version silently.

# 104. Repository direction

The current repository contains a generic notification domain with:

* Notification type;
* title;
* body;
* optional deal reference;
* creation time;
* read state.

That is sufficient for a basic event inbox, but not for Tasks & Approvals.

The current notification types are limited to:

* Deal;
* engine;
* document;
* system.

There is no complete canonical model for:

* Assignment;
* due date;
* task progress;
* target object;
* evidence;
* authority;
* approval stages;
* decisions;
* conditions;
* invalidation;
* escalation.

## Keep

* Existing user and deal identity;
* notification delivery;
* audit concepts;
* role and permission direction;
* agent workflow suspension;
* deal-scoped objects;
* top-bar work count;
* exact deep-link architecture.

## Add

* Work-item domain;
* task and review lifecycles;
* approval-request domain;
* authority policies;
* sequential and parallel approvals;
* dependencies;
* deadline and escalation rules;
* decision history;
* version locking;
* conditional approvals;
* external-approval routing;
* team queue;
* work-item search and filters.

## Do not overload notifications

Notifications may announce:

> Approval assigned to you.

The approval itself should remain a separate durable object.

Marking the notification read must not affect the approval.

## Agent integration

Human-in-the-loop suspension should create an `ApprovalRequest`.

The workflow may resume only after:

* A valid decision;
* correct authority;
* matching object version;
* satisfied conditions, where required.

# 105. What not to include

Avoid:

* Notification and task objects merged together;
* blind bulk approval;
* employee productivity rankings;
* one unexplained priority score;
* automatically completed tasks after upload;
* self-approval without policy;
* hidden authority thresholds;
* mutable approval decisions;
* decision against stale object versions;
* approval based only on a one-line summary;
* automatically approved agent output;
* dismissed notifications completing work;
* duplicated work items;
* reassignment to unauthorized users;
* due-date changes without audit;
* recurring reminder spam;
* restricted evidence in the queue preview;
* raw workflow-engine status;
* technical queue identifiers;
* fake live tasks in production states;
* real customer or counterparty names in pitch fixtures.

# 121. Design validation criteria

The screen is ready for approval when:

1. Tasks, reviews and approvals are visibly distinct.
2. Notifications remain separate from actionable work.
3. Every work item explains why it exists.
4. Every item links to an exact deal object.
5. Transaction consequence appears before generic metadata.
6. Prioritization is deterministic and explainable.
7. Materiality, severity, urgency and priority remain distinct.
8. Every work item has an eligible owner.
9. Unassigned critical work is surfaced.
10. Delegation preserves accountable ownership.
11. Approval authority is explicit.
12. Unauthorized users cannot decide.
13. Separation-of-duties rules are enforceable.
14. Multi-stage and parallel approvals are supported.
15. Conditional approvals create controlled follow-up work.
16. Decisions are immutable.
17. Decisions are tied to exact object versions.
18. Source changes can invalidate decisions.
19. Historical approvals remain auditable.
20. Duplicate work items can be detected.
21. Dismissing a notification cannot complete work.
22. Uploading evidence cannot automatically satisfy a review.
23. Material decisions cannot be blindly bulk-approved.
24. Focus Review preserves complete context.
25. External participants see only restricted decision context.
26. Agent workflows suspend at approval boundaries.
27. Workflow resume requires a valid persisted decision.
28. Overdue and blocked remain distinct.
29. Deadline changes are audited.
30. Restricted information does not leak through queue previews.
31. Team Queue avoids employee scoring and gamification.
32. The screen remains useful without Copilot.
33. Mobile supports review, delegation and approval.
34. Partial failures do not hide the remaining queue.
35. Concurrent decisions use object-version checks.
36. The pitch mock remains fictional.
37. The static composition remains readable.
38. The product demonstrates human governance rather than approval theater.

# 122. Final design target

The correct design target is not:

> “A unified inbox for notifications and to-dos.”

It is:

> **A cross-deal decision workspace where every required human action is tied to its evidence, authority, deadline, transaction consequence and immutable audit record.**

Its defining interaction is:

> **Identify the required action → understand why it matters → inspect the evidence → open the exact transaction context → complete or decide → propagate the authorized consequence**
